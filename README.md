## CS 250 – Software Development Lifecycle

This section includes my Sprint Review and Retrospective for the SNHU Travel project and my reflection on software development and teamwork.

### How do I interpret user needs and implement them into a program? How does creating user stories help with this?

I start by figuring out what the user wants to accomplish and why it matters to them. A request can sound simple but still leave out details, so asking questions before writing code can prevent wasted work. User stories help turn those requests into smaller goals by explaining who needs a feature, what they need, and why. For the SNHU Travel project, this meant thinking about how someone would browse vacation options and what information would help them choose a trip. Acceptance criteria make those stories easier to implement and test because they describe what the finished feature needs to do.

### How do I approach developing programs? What Agile processes do I hope to incorporate into my future development work?

My approach is to break the program into smaller parts, work on one piece at a time, and test as I go. I am still building my programming skills, and smaller steps make it easier to find mistakes and understand what needs to change. In future projects, I want to use a prioritized backlog and short development cycles to keep the work manageable. I also want to use reviews to get feedback before going too far in one direction. Retrospectives would help me look at how I worked and choose specific improvements for the next round.

### What does it mean to be a good team member in software development?

Being a good team member means doing my part while keeping other people informed. If I get stuck or fall behind, I need to speak up early because someone else's work might depend on mine. It also means listening to feedback, asking questions when something is unclear, and being willing to help with problems outside my own tasks. Clear documentation and readable code make it easier for others to work with what I have done. To me, a dependable teammate takes responsibility for mistakes and helps the group move toward its goal.
